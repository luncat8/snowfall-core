before the train · pages without art

Raw text, with no paragraph tag around it, sitting directly inside the section — the engine's free notation, and a stop of its own.

This chapter has no art wagon, so its pages can be as small as the prose in them.
The line break is a stop too: the tap that lands here stops between the two lines, so the second is still a line away.
So a tap does not only end a paragraph — it can end a sentence, or a line, wherever the author put a stop.
And what is below a page this short is not the text you have read: it is blank paper, as long as the window needs to be able to reach the page.

A page is the author's own portion between two stops, and nothing else: what came before is above the reader, not on the page, and what comes next is below the paper's edge, not in the document at all.

events: —

1 · the announcement

The lamp above the platform has been dead since the winter began, and the porter keeps it lit anyway — one bulb, one chair, and a rule nobody wrote down.

She reads the board the way other people read weather. Nothing is coming. That is not the same as nothing being written down, and the difference is the whole of your evening. You have eleven minutes before the barrier lifts.

2 · the crossing

Two doors. The left one is warm and smells of wet wool; the right opens on a stair that goes down for a long time and smells of nothing at all. Pick one, or walk past: the engine records the answer either way — a skipped answer replays your own, and one you never reach pays the declared default.

The door you chose is unopened, and the porter has stopped pretending to look at the board.

3 · the tally

The train that does not come is still a train. It has a number, a length, and a habit of arriving at four in the morning for people who no longer live here.

The purse is …. It is a pure function of the save: the engine records every outcome — live, saved or default — and this page only draws it.
