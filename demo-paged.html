before the train · how the pages fall

Raw text, with no paragraph tag around it, sits directly inside the section — the engine's free notation, and a portion of its own.
A page is one screenful. The author's stops are where a page may end, and the page ends at the last stop that fits the screen — so this line break is a candidate, not a promise: the page ends here only if the screen does.
Short portions share a screen. A tap does not hop one line at a time: it fills the window with whole portions and turns at a stop near the bottom edge. Nothing is ever cut mid-portion.

What came before is behind the reader — above the page, not on it. What comes next is below the paper's edge: unpainted, unreachable, and waiting for its own screen. When the story's own rhythm leaves a page short, the rest of the screen is blank paper, never the text you have already read.

events: —

1 · the announcement

The lamp above the platform has been dead since the winter began, and the porter keeps it lit anyway — one bulb, one chair, and a rule nobody wrote down.

She reads the board the way other people read weather. Nothing is coming. That is not the same as nothing being written down, and the difference is the whole of your evening. You have eleven minutes before the barrier lifts.
