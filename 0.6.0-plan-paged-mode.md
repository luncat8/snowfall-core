# 0.6.0 — Paged mode (read like a visual novel)

## Goal

The same story reads two ways from one DOM. Book mode scrolls the whole text.
Paged mode shows **one screenful of whole portions a page**: the author's own
stops (`p`, `section`, `br`) are where a page may end, the page ends at the
last stop that fits the screen, and the text already read leaves the page.
Nothing else changes — the engine, the wagons, the events and the games are
the same in both modes.

## The model

A **portion** is the text between two consecutive stops — the author's own
unit. A **page** is a group of consecutive portions filling one screen:

- the page ends at the last stop whose portion end still fits the reading
  band (the window minus the host's fixed chrome); short portions share a
  screen, and the page never ends mid-portion;
- when nothing fits — the next portion is taller than the band — that
  portion is the page on its own. A tall portion is the author's choice to
  write past the screen; the reader scrolls it natively and taps walk it one
  band a tap. Its page is never skipped and never teleports to its end;
- a page shorter than the band is padded with blank paper under its text —
  never with the text that came before.

The reader opens every page on its own first portion, at the top of the
reading band (`padTop` under a fixed toolbar); the text before the page is
above the window, behind the reader. A page is read at its last window —
which is also the document's maximum scroll, so "parked at the top()" is
"this page is done".

Cover art is a window (`cover` wagons are `100vh` tall by engine contract),
so a page over one sizes its portion to the full window: the art is never
sliced by a page edge and the page is walked like any tall portion.

## The mechanism: reveal by geometry, not by DOM

Paged mode moves no story node. It clamps the story root's own height to the
current page and clips the block axis (`height` + `overflow-y: clip` — never
`overflow-x`, which would kill sticky positioning and change line boxes).
What the reader sees is the whole real document, scrolled to the page. Wagons
keep their boxes, anchors keep their Ys, `exportJSON` stays byte-identical
across the switch, and book mode is one style write away.

## Boundaries

`measure()` reads the story's cut list and groups it into pages:

1. `readBox()` — the root's unclamped box (the clamp is off for the read) and
   the out-of-flow paints' low-water mark;
2. `walk()` — one flat descent; a stop is a candidate boundary at its own
   top (`br` at the gap between the lines around it, via `Range`), and a
   candidate is kept as a cut only once its portion has content — `</p>`
   margin then `<p>`, `<br><br>`, a `<br>` before `</p>`, a `<section>`
   opening straight into its first `<p>` all collapse into the one page they
   really are. Two cuts within a pixel are one. The last cut is the root's
   unclamped bottom edge, so the final page reveals the whole story;
3. `groupPages()` — the screenful rule above: fill from the previous page's
   end, take the last cut that fits the band, or the tall portion alone.

The list only grows its buffers; a re-measure re-anchors the reader by
element, not by index.

## Stepping

- `next()` — if the page is not all read yet (taller than the band), hurry
  one band per tap toward the page's done spot; when it is read, turn to the
  next page and open it at its own start. The end of the story returns
  `false`. A glide in flight counts at its committed target, so
  `while (P.next());` advances one band a call and lands.
- `prev()` — go to the previous page's start; the clamp grows back only after
  the scroll is there (deferred shrink), so nothing jumps. A page taller than
  the band is entered at its top — the natural back position.
- arrivals scroll to `pageOpen`; the "done" spot (`top()`) is only checked,
  never targeted by a turn.

## Input

One pointer tap (not on interactive chrome — links, buttons, seats, host
`ignore` selectors) or Space turns; Shift+Space / Backspace goes back. Arrow
keys stay native scrolling — a walked portion is scrolled the usual way.

## API — `window.SnowfallPaged`

`set(on, fromTop)`, `get()`, `next()`, `prev()`, `index`, `count`, `y[i]`
(page end Ys), `at(i)` (the stop element page `i` ends at; the story root for
the last), `top()` (the current page's done scroll), `cutN`, `cutY[i]`,
`cutAt(i)` (the walk's portion cuts — the author's units), `root`,
`padTop`/`padBottom` (fixed chrome, px), `stops`, `smooth`, `ignore`,
`version`. Everything the old surface had, plus the cut list.

## Test matrix

- node gate (`node test/paged.js`): the cut rules, the screenful grouping,
  `pageOpen`/`pageRead`/`band`, tall-page stepping, deferred shrink,
  re-anchoring, source edits, padding, the read-only API.
- browser gate (`test/browser/check.js`): the clamp moves no wagon and no
  anchor; every page fills its screen of whole portions or is one portion;
  every arrival opens on the page's own real cut element at the band top;
  no page shows earlier text; short pages are blank paper; tall portions are
  walked; no cover art is sliced by a page edge; keys and taps; the demo's
  own self-test; `file://` boot.
- demo self-test: the same invariants in-page, against live rects.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A short story is just itself — its pages are as big as its prose, the
   last page ends at the story's end, and there is no blank filler page.
2. A screenful of short paragraphs is one page; it ends at the last stop
   that fits, and the rest of the screen is blank paper only when the next
   portion cannot fit.
3. A page over one portion (a cover card, a long speech) is walked one band
   a tap, and back enters it at its top.
4. The first line of text always clears the fixed toolbar.
5. Art on a page reaches the page's edges — never half a cover.
6. Toggling book ⇄ paged moves no wagon, no event anchor, and lands on the
   same place of the story.

## Pitfalls (the ones that bit)

- **The clamp-off measure moves the reader.** `measure()` must read the root
  unclamped; with the paper gone the browser drops the scroll to the shorter
  document's end *synchronously*, and a walk that measures per tap then
  hurries lost ground forever. Take the scroll before the clamp comes off
  and put it back after.
- **A tight `while (P.next());` over a smooth scroll** re-hurries a position
  the glide has left. Count a glide at its committed target (`P.going`).
- **Fakedom proportions mislead**: portions of a quarter screen merge into
  one 800px page; tests that need pages must use portions over half a band.
- **Cover wagons are `100vh`** (engine contract). With `padTop` the reading
  band is shorter, so a window-tall portion page is walked two taps — that
  is the design; shrinking the art instead would fight the engine.
- **`min-height` boxes around single paragraphs** make one-portion pages with
  a screen of blank inside the portion. Fill screens are several short
  paragraphs; the grouper packs them.

## Decisions

- The page unit is **the screenful**, not one portion per page: one-portion
  pages leave blank paper on every screen and slivers at every short stop.
- Greedy "last stop that fits" (not best-fit): the chosen option text is
  literal, and a bounded blank before a tall portion is accepted over
  pulling a tall portion up and burying its neighbors.
- `br` cuts at the line gap (midpoint of the rects around it), not at the
  glyph edge and not at the `br` rect's own bottom.
- The end cut is the unclamped root bottom — the last page must reveal
  everything, including story padding and trailing whitespace.
- Re-anchoring is keep-by-element; an arrival is always `pageOpen` (the
  page's own start), `pageRead` is the done spot and the document's max
  scroll.
