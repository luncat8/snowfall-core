# 0.6.0 — Paged mode (read like a visual novel)

## Goal

The same story page, read one portion at a time. A tap, a click or a key
turns to the next portion of text and brings it to the top of the window;
everything already read is above the reader and off the page, everything not
yet read is not on the page. Backgrounds park and push, colours morph, scripts fire, prompts and
minigames appear — all exactly as in book mode, because the reading position
still moves through the same document and the engine never learns which mode
is on. A portion ends where the author's own markup ends it: at the next
`<p>`, the next `<section>` or the next `<br>` — the stop tags. The reader
switches between book and paged mode at any time and keeps the position.

Depends on: 0.2 (wagons), 0.3 (morph), 0.4/0.4.1 (events, `ask`), 0.5.5
(`Snowfall.viewport`), 0.4.3 (games are content like any other). Adds one
optional file, `snowfall-paged.js`, and no engine geometry change.

## The mechanism decision: reveal by geometry, not by DOM

**A page is one portion: the author's own text between two stops, and
nothing else.** Paged mode is three style writes on the story root:
`height: <boundary>px` ends the portion, `overflow-y: clip` keeps everything
past it unpainted, and a bottom `margin` gives a portion too short to fill
the window the room it needs to sit at the top of the window. Turning a page
raises the height to the next stop and scrolls so that the *new portion's own
start* is the window's top edge: the text already read is above the reader
and off the page, and the text after the stop is below the paper's edge,
unpainted and unreachable. Nothing inside the root moves: `overflow-y: clip`
is not a scroll container, so `position: sticky` wagons keep the viewport as
their scrollport and park at `top: 0` as before, and every anchor keeps its
document Y.

Measured in Chromium on the harness page (`#n=3&bgs=1&realScenes=1`,
1280×800) before writing this plan: after `height = Y; overflow-y = clip` on
`#app`, `Snowfall.wagons.y` and `Snowfall.events.y` are byte-identical to
before, `scrollHeight` becomes `round(Y)`, scrolling to the end leaves the
boundary at `799.6px` from the top, and the current wagon reports `pos 0` with
a painted rect of `0..800` — parked, unclipped. Adding
`margin-bottom = B` then extends the document to `round(Y + B)` and paints
nothing in the margin: `elementFromPoint` there returns `HTML`, and the
reader can be put exactly at `Y − vh`. That is the whole premise;
`test/browser/check.js` re-proves it on every run (see Test matrix).

Why not the previous approach — walking the DOM element by element and
showing everything up to the next stop tag — and its relatives:

| approach | why not |
|---|---|
| walk the DOM, `display: none` on everything after the stop | Removing boxes from layout moves every anchor and wagon below, so each step needs a `refresh()`, the parked wagon's parent bottom changes under it, and the event thresholds are measured against a document that keeps changing height. "Works the same as book mode" is lost by construction. |
| `visibility: hidden` per element | Keeps layout, but a text node cannot be half hidden: a `<br>` splits inline content *inside* one element, and the free notation the engine promises — text nodes, `<br>`, `<span>`s, `<em>`s between stop tags — puts stop tags at every depth. |
| wrap each run between stops in a `<span>` at measure | Handles `<br>` but rewrites the author's DOM: a run that crosses an inline element needs one wrapper per sibling group, the source editor must strip them on every round trip, and no-JS purity is gone. The engine already inserts marker elements, but only zero-size ones. |
| `clip-path: inset()` on the root | Geometric like the chosen one, but it does not shorten the document: the reader can scroll into blank space, and anchors below the boundary cross their thresholds and fire for text nobody has seen. A JS scroll clamp on top of it fights wheel and touch momentum. |
| a VN text box that extracts the current portion | A second rendering of the text that the wagons, morph and events know nothing about; the "same story, same engine" property is the point of this repo. |

The height clamp is O(1) per step, needs no knowledge of what lies between
two stops, and leaves the author's markup untouched.

## Non-goals

- No text box overlay, no typewriter reveal, no per-character timing, no
  transitions beyond the browser's own smooth scroll.
- No auto-advance timer, no backlog UI, no "skip read text" — the reader can
  scroll up through revealed text as in a book.
- No persistence of the page index. Browser scroll restoration plus
  `set(true)` at that position lands on the same page.
- No change to event, store or `ask` semantics. Advancing past a pending
  prompt abandons it exactly as scrolling past does in book mode (see
  Decisions).
- No engine geometry, no new scroll listener, no rAF loop, no changes to the
  layout contract.

## Authoring contract

- **Stop tags** end a portion: `p`, `section`, `br`. A stop element *starts*
  a new page at its top edge (`p`, `section`) or after its line (`br`).
  Override per page with `data-stops="p,section,br,hr"` on the story root.
- **Excluded subtrees**: anything inside `.snow-bg`, `.snow-stick`,
  `.snow-game`, `.snow-prompt`, `script`, `style`, and any element carrying
  `data-nopage`. A `<br>` in a caption or in a game's painted UI is never a
  page break.
- **The story root** is the engine's scope (`#app` or the element passed to
  `create({scope})`). It must be a normal block in vertical flow that ends
  the document: no in-flow content after it, no bottom border or margin of
  its own (vertical padding is fine — the clamp arithmetic accounts for it,
  and `overflow: clip` cuts at the padding box, which is where the boundary
  is put). One warning per measure when the rule is broken. Floats or
  absolutely positioned children that hang below their portion are clipped
  with it — story content is a vertical flow, as the layout contract
  already says.
- **Default mode**: `<html data-paged>` (or `<meta name="paged" content="1">`)
  boots in paged mode from the first portion; otherwise book mode. The host
  persists the reader's choice however it likes (the harness uses its hash).
- **No JS / engine absent**: the file is inert; the page is the readable
  prose it always was. `data-paged` without the engine changes nothing.

## Boundaries (measured at `measure()`)

The controller is a subscriber: `Snowfall.use({ measure, frame })`. All
layout reads happen in `measure`; `frame` does one comparison (below).

1. Walk the root's element tree in document order with `firstChild` /
   `nextSibling` (not `TreeWalker` — the fake DOM has neither, and the walk
   is trivial), skipping excluded subtrees whole. Collect every stop element
   into `P.el` (array, reused) and the text-bearing state between stops: a
   portion is **non-empty** if a non-blank text node (or a wagon marker —
   `.snow-bg` counts as content so a picture-only portion is its own page)
   was seen since the previous kept stop.
2. **Y of a stop**, document coordinates (`rect + scrollY`):
   - `p`, `section` (and any block stop): `getBoundingClientRect().top`.
   - `br`: the boundary between its line and the next — the midpoint of the
     `br` rect's bottom and the top of the first client rect of the next
     non-blank text after it (`Range` over its first character). No such
     text, or no `Range` (fake DOM): the `br` rect's bottom. Chromium puts
     the `br` rect on the content area of the line it ends (16px text in a
     26px line: `29..48`, next line's glyphs at `55`), so the midpoint `51.5`
     is the line box edge — glyphs of the current line stay whole, nothing
     of the next line shows.
   - An element without a rect (`display: none`) is skipped.
3. **Merge**: a stop whose portion is empty is dropped (`</p>` margin then
   `<p>`, `<br><br>`, a `<br>` right before `</p>`, a `<section>` opening
   straight into its first `<p>`). Two kept boundaries closer than 1px
   collapse into the later one. Y is non-decreasing after this; a decrease
   (out-of-flow markup) drops the earlier of the pair with one warning.
4. **End boundary**: the root's content bottom is always the last entry, so
   the last page reveals everything and `set(false)` is a plain style reset.
5. State, preallocated and grown like the engine's arrays:
   `P = { n, el: [], y: Float64Array, k: -1, kEl: null, padBoxTop, padTop,
   padBottom, borderTop, borderBottom, borderBox, pendingH: -1,
   pendingAt: 0 }`. `k` is the current page: content is
   revealed up to `y[k]`. Across a re-measure the page is kept by element
   identity (`kEl`), not by index — a source edit above the reader must not
   move the reader off the page they were on.

## Reveal and stepping

Definitions: `vh = Snowfall.viewport.height`, `sY = window.scrollY`,
`start(k) = k ? y[k−1] : 0` (where page `k`'s portion begins),
`band(k) = max(0, vh − (y[k] − start(k)))` (the blank paper a short page
needs under it), `spot(k) = max(start(k), y[k] − vh)` (where page `k` is
read: its own top when the portion fits the window, its last window when the
portion is taller — which is also the document's maximum scroll, so a page is
fully read the moment the reader is there), and `stepMax = 1` (fraction of
`vh` one step may scroll).

- **`apply(k)`**: put the root's padding-box bottom on `y[k]`:
  `pb = y[k] − padBoxTop` (`padBoxTop` = border-box top + top border, read
  once per measure), then `root.style.height = pb − padTop − padBottom` for
  `box-sizing: content-box` or `pb + borderTop + borderBottom` for
  `border-box`, and `root.style.overflowY = 'clip'`, then
  `root.style.marginBottom = band(k) + 'px'` (or `''` when the band is 0,
  written only when it changes, so a steady page costs two style writes).
  Nothing else is touched — the *shorthand* is never used, so the author's
  `overflow-x` and bottom margin survive until paged mode replaces them.
  The document is then exactly `y[k] + band(k)` tall and its maximum scroll
  is exactly `spot(k)`: a short portion is reachable at its own top, a tall
  one at its last window. The band lives outside the clip, so it paints the
  page's own background and nothing else — the unrevealed text under it is
  still unpainted and still unreachable.
- **`set(true)`** at any position: `k = first i with y[i] ≥ sY + vh` (else
  `n − 1`), `apply(k)`, scroll unchanged. Nothing visible disappears and
  nothing moves. **`set(true, fromTop)`** — boot with `data-paged`, or the
  demo's "restart" — uses `k = 0` at `sY = 0`: the first page is the first
  portion alone, and the rest of the window is the blank band beneath it.
- **`set(false)`**: remove the three inline properties. Scroll unchanged,
  document back to full height, engine state identical (same `sY`, same
  anchors).
- **`next()`**:
  1. `here = spot(k)`. If `sY < here − 1` (the reader is inside a page
     taller than the window, or a previous step is still travelling): scroll
     to `min(here, sY + stepMax·vh)` and return `true` — no reveal. A quick
     second tap hurries to the end of the page; it never skips a page.
  2. If `k === n − 1`: return `false` (end of story).
  3. `k += 1`, `apply(k)`, scroll to `min(spot(k), sY + stepMax·vh)`,
     return `true`. A turn is never longer than one window — the reader is
     at the old page's spot and the new page starts at most a window below
     it — so a turn is always one tap; the cap only bites on a page taller
     than the window, which is then walked a window at a time, and it is
     what keeps events honest: no anchor can travel from below the window to
     above it in one jump, so `skip` never fires for a page the reader is
     looking at.
- **`prev()`**: if `k === 0` return `false`. `k -= 1` (and `kEl` with it —
  every path that sets `k` sets `kEl`), scroll to
  `spot(k)`, and remember `pendingH = k`, `pendingEl` (the pending page's
  own stop element) and `pendingAt = spot(k)`; the
  `frame(sY)` hook applies the height and the band when
  `sY ≤ pendingAt + 1` (or at once when the scroll is instant), resolving
  the pending page by element, never by a list index a re-walk may have
  shifted. Shrinking
  first would make the browser clamp the scroll instantly and the page
  would jump.
- **Scrolling** uses one reused options object, `{ top, behavior }`;
  `behavior` is `'smooth'` unless `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` or
  `SnowfallPaged.smooth === 0` (QA sets it to compare logs deterministically).
  The engine sees ordinary scroll events either way.
- **`measure()` while paged**: re-walk, re-find `kEl` (fallback: nearest
  boundary at or below the old `y[k]`), `apply(k)`. Fonts, resize and source
  edits keep the reader on the same page. **And if the re-measure moved the
  page, the reader moves with it** (`anchor(k, oldLo)`): the reading place
  inside the page is preserved (`at + shift`, clamped into
  `[start(k), spot(k)]`), a reader above the page's old start is re-reading
  and is taken to the page's new start, a settled reader is snapped (the
  layout under them already jumped) and a turn still travelling is
  re-targeted (a second smooth `scrollTo` restarts the glide from wherever
  the browser left it). Skipped while a `prev()` glide is pending — the
  arrival owns the scroll.
- **The list is never allowed to grow old.** The story changes its own flow
  height while the reader is on it — a minigame seats itself the first time
  its anchor is looked at and unseats when the reader walks away — and a
  boundary list that no longer matches the document turns every page after
  it: the text before the portion stays on top of the window, the paper cuts
  the portion early. Two guards: every turn and every toggle re-measures (a
  tap is rare, the walk is cheap, and a host that drives the controller
  synchronously cannot wait for a tick); and the root's subtree is watched
  with a `MutationObserver` — `childList` only, because the engine paints
  its wagons with style writes and the clamp is a style write too — whose
  one coalesced measure on the next tick re-anchors a reader parked on a
  page that moved under them.
- **Reader scrolling** stays native inside the revealed region — wheel,
  touch, keys the controller does not claim. Below the page's paper nothing
  unrevealed is reachable and no hidden anchor fires. Above it the text that
  has been read is still there: wheeling up re-reads it, exactly as `prev()`
  does deliberately, because paged mode never destroys the story, it only
  decides which portion is on the page.

## Input

Claimed only while paged mode is on:

- **Tap / click**: `pointerdown` records position, time, pointer count;
  `pointerup` within 8px and 500ms, primary button, one active pointer,
  collapsed selection → `next()`. Ignored when the target is inside `a,
  button, input, select, textarea, label, summary, [contenteditable],
  .snow-prompt, .snow-game, .snow-region-hud` or any element with
  `data-nopage`, when `SnowfallRegion.getInspect()` is true (the reader is
  panning a picture), or when the harness panel/drawers contain the target
  (`#panel, #diag, #src` — the host passes an `ignore` selector).
- **Keys**: `Space`, `Enter`, `ArrowRight`, `PageDown` → `next()`;
  `Backspace`, `ArrowLeft`, `PageUp` → `prev()`. `ArrowUp`/`ArrowDown` stay
  native scrolling. Nothing is claimed with a modifier held or with focus in
  an editable element. `preventDefault` only on the keys claimed.
- Handlers are attached once at load (`window`, passive where possible) and
  are no-ops in book mode; they never read layout.

## API

```js
SnowfallPaged.set(on, fromTop)   // toggle; keeps position (fromTop: start at page 0)
SnowfallPaged.get()              // true while paged
SnowfallPaged.next()  /  SnowfallPaged.prev()   // true when something moved
SnowfallPaged.index              // current page k          (read-only)
SnowfallPaged.count              // boundaries incl. the end (read-only)
SnowfallPaged.y                  // Float64Array of boundaries, valid until refresh
SnowfallPaged.at(i)              // the stop element boundary i came from (the root for the last)
SnowfallPaged.root               // the story root the controller clamps
SnowfallPaged.top()              // where this page is read: its portion's start, or its last window
SnowfallPaged.stops              // 'p,section,br' — the effective list
SnowfallPaged.smooth             // 1 | 0
SnowfallPaged.ignore             // extra selector the host wants taps ignored in
SnowfallPaged.version            // '0.7.0', the controller's own
```

The five getters are defined with a no-op setter as well, so a stray
assignment in sloppy code is ignored instead of shadowing the state; the
node gate asserts it. `module.exports` guarded, `require()`-able in node
with no DOM, inert when `Snowfall.default` is absent (a page with the file
but no engine).

  1. boundary count equals a DOM count of stop tags outside excluded
     subtrees minus the empty portions; `y` strictly increasing; last entry
     is the root bottom ±1;
  2. `set(true)` at three scroll positions: `scrollY`, `Snowfall.wagons.y`,
     `Snowfall.events.y` and the parked wagon's rect unchanged, no `.ln`
     that was inside the window is now beyond the document end;
  3. stepping from the top with `smooth = 0` until `next()` returns false:
     after every step `scrollHeight === round(y[k] + band(k))` and
     `scrollY ≤ spot(k) + 1`, every step moved at most `stepMax·vh + 1`,
     and the
     `Snowlog` sequence (chapter, event) equals the slow-scroll sequence
     `qEvents` recorded — including `parked` — with zero `skip`;
  4. `prev()` back to page 0 fires nothing new (reverse), heights follow;
  5. toggling off restores `scrollHeight` to the book value, toggling on
     again lands on the same `k`;
  6. `$('src').value` identical before and after the whole run — no DOM
     mutation.
- The diagnostics panel shows `page k/n · portion y` while paged.

## Demo page — `demo-paged.html`

Self-contained like `demo-minigame.html`, and its page rhythm is part of the
demonstration, because a page is the author's own portion between two stops:
a text-only opening chapter (no art wagon) with a raw text run, a

them, padded with blank paper; then three art chapters, each a full-window
gradient cover, a full-window chapter card, full-window paragraphs and one
paragraph taller than the window that has to be walked a screen at a time.
A `book / paged` toggle button plus the `v` key, a `restart` (page 0 at top),
a live `page k/n · Npx of text on this page` readout, and a self-test button
that runs the checks in the page and prints PASS/FAIL lines. Loads
`snowfall.js`, `snowfall-paged.js`, `snowfall-games.js`, `games/gamble.js`,
one shared `?v=` token, `file://` friendly.

## Test matrix

**Node — `test/paged.js`** (fake DOM, `buildPage()`; the fake lays out from a
`measure(node, vh, inner)` model, so line geometry is the model's, not the
browser's, and the `<br>` midpoint rule is a browser-only check):

- stop walk: `section`, `p` and `br` at three depths; a heading is not a stop;
  a `br` inside `.snow-bg` and `[data-nopage]` never counts, and neither does
  a `<script>`; `data-stops="p,hr"` makes an `<hr>` with prose after it a
  page on that page only;
- merges: empty `<p>`, `<br><br>`, a trailing `<br>` before `</p>`, a
  `<section>` opening into `<p>`, a blank opening page; a picture-only
  portion is *kept* (a wagon counts as content); an out-of-flow stop that
  would move the list backwards is warned about exactly once;
- end boundary is the story root's bottom edge; `y` is non-decreasing; a
  section whose only content is a game box merges into the next page;
- `set(true)` at four positions (0, 120, 400, the end): `k` is the first
  boundary ≥ `sY + vh`; `style.height` puts the padding box on `y[k]` for
  `content-box` and `border-box` with padding *and* border; `overflow-y` is
  `clip` and `overflow-x` is never written; `style.marginBottom` is the band
  and is written only when it changes; `set(false)` clears all three;
- `next()`: one portion a tap; a page taller than the window is walked
  (hurried) a window at a time and revealed only at its last line; the
  scroll never passes `spot(k)` or `stepMax·vh`, and never lands above
  `start(k)`; the end of the story returns false. `prev()`: the height is held until the scroll
  arrives (`pendingH` + the frame hook), and with `smooth = 0` it shrinks
  first; back to page 0, then false;
- re-measure after inserting a paragraph above the reader keeps the same stop
  element on the page, and the boundary that moved is the reader's;
- the story changes its own height (the fake observer + timer a browser
  provides are the gate's to fire): a burst of mutations schedules one
  coalesced measure; the walk keeps the reader's page by element and moves
  the reader with it, so no earlier text is on the page; a reader wheeled
  above their page is put on its new start; a burst whose tick has not run
  still cannot stale a turn — `next()` re-measures first; the watch, its
  tick and the re-anchor read no layout (the walk does the reading);
- input: taps inside the host's `ignore` selector, on a link, a drag, a
  pointerup with no pointerdown, a slow press, `ArrowDown`, a held key, a
  shortcut and a key aimed at a link are all left to the browser; `Space`,
  `Backspace`, `PageDown/PageUp` and `←/→` turn the page and are prevented;
  in book mode nothing is claimed at all;
- no engine on the page (only `snowfall-paged.js` loaded): `set`, `next`,
  `prev` are quiet falses, `count` is 0, nothing is written to the page, and
  no call throws;
- the controller's source contains no `addEventListener('scroll'`, no
  `requestAnimationFrame`, no `overflow` shorthand, and no layout read
  (`getBoundingClientRect`, `getComputedStyle`, `getClientRects`, offsets)

**Browser — `test/browser/paged.js`** (Chromium, self-served, on
`demo-paged.html` at 1280×800; loaded by `test/browser/check.js`):

- boot: `data-paged` starts paged with N portions, the document is exactly

  park at the window top with the whole window as its box, and the page's

  of the pass no wagon's box straddles the page's edge;

  glyph rect of the line before and the first glyph rect after, at least 2px

  the window; after every tap `scrollHeight == round(y[k] + band(k))`, the
  reader is never past `spot(k)` and never above `start(k)` — so no page ever
  shows text from before its own portion — never more than `1·vh` per step,
  never backwards; a short page opens on its own first line with blank paper
  (`elementFromPoint` outside the story root) under it, a tall one is read
  at its last line; the end returns false; back taps walk to page 1;
  and every arrival is measured against the live document, not against the
  controller's own list — at an arrival on a page that fits the window, the
  window top is the page's own stop element's real top (`at(k−1)`'s
  `getBoundingClientRect().top`), which is what catches a story that seats a
  minigame above the reader mid-walk;
- input: `Space` advances, `Backspace` goes back, `ArrowDown` does not; taps
  on `#bar`, a prompt button, a game seat and a link do not turn the page,
  a tap on the prose does;
- the page's own self-test prints no FAIL;
- `file://`: boots paged with the first portion, padded to one window.

## Pitfalls

- `overflow: hidden` on the root would make it a scroll container and break
  every sticky wagon; only `clip` keeps the viewport as the scrollport. The
  layout contract's overflow rule stands — `clip` is its one exception, the
  same one the host already uses for lateral exits.
- Never hide by layout. Any `display: none` or height change *inside* the
  root moves anchors and needs a `refresh()`; the clamp on the root moves
  nothing.
- `scrollHeight` is an integer while boundaries are fractional: a page's
  window edge sits within a pixel of `start(k)` or `spot(k)`, so every gate
  compares with a 1.5px tolerance and the `<br>` rule uses the line
  midpoint, never a glyph edge.
- A `<br>` rect in Chromium is the content area, not the line box; a
  boundary at `br.bottom` would show the next line's half-leading only, but
  the midpoint with the next glyph rect is exact and font-independent.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` makes every step a jump; the `stepMax` cap is what
  keeps `skip` from firing in that case — do not raise it above `1 −
  hysteresis/vh`.
- A second `scrollTo({behavior:'smooth'})` while one is in flight restarts
  the animation from the current position; `next()` computes from `k`, not
  from `scrollY`, so an early tap hurries instead of double-advancing.
- iOS elastic overscroll can report `scrollY` beyond the maximum for a
  frame; the arrived-at comparisons use `≥ spot − 1`, never equality.
- The fake DOM's rects have zero height: the node gate covers walk, merge
  and step arithmetic; line geometry belongs to the browser gate only.

## Decisions

- **The window's top on the portion's start, and a blank band under a short
  page.** The bottom edge is the only place where nothing visible is ever
  clipped, but it is also where the text you have already read stays on the
  page, which is not paged reading. Putting the new portion at the top hides
  the old text by putting it above the reader; the cost is that a portion
  shorter than the window needs the document to reach the window's top, so
  the root carries a bottom margin and the paper under a short page is
  empty. The band is margin, never padding (padding is inside the clip and
  would paint the unrevealed text) and never a spacer element (the author's
  DOM is not ours to change). See the review for the three mechanisms
  measured and rejected before this one.
- **Advancing past a pending prompt abandons it**, as scrolling past does.
  A mandatory hold would trap the reader in a game with no walk-away button
  (the gamble has none by design), and a two-tap escape is a second rule
  for the same gesture. Prompts stay on screen while the reader is on the
  page, so a reader who wants to answer can; "all work the same" was the
  brief.
- **Page kept by element, not index**, across re-measure: source edits above
  the reader are the harness's daily case.
- **The boundary list is measured against the live document at the moment it
  is used, not cached until the next refresh.** A story with minigames
  changes its own flow height while the reader is on it — a game seats
  itself the first time its anchor is looked at, unseats when the reader
  walks away — and nothing calls `Snowfall.refresh()` for that (the games
  runner deliberately does not: a refresh would drop every live session).
  A cached list therefore goes stale mid-walk, and the engine's own refresh
  cannot even fix it: subscribers measure in registration order, and the
  games runner's `dropAll` mutates the tree *after* the controller's
  measure has run. So a turn re-measures (a tap is rare, the walk is cheap,
  and a synchronous host cannot wait for a tick), and a subtree watch
  re-anchors the parked reader on the next tick. The reader moves with
  their page — the reading place inside the page is preserved — because a
  page that slid down under a seated game otherwise puts the text before
  the portion back on top of the window, which is the opposite of paged
  reading.
- **`p`, `section`, `br` by default; more via `data-stops`.** The three the
  user named cover prose; headings end at the `<p>` that follows them and
  make a natural title page.

## Review after implementation

What the code does now, where it differs from the plan above, and why.

**The premise held, and it is the whole design.** The clamp is three inline
writes on the story root and nothing else; in Chromium, at the same
`scrollY`, every wagon paints a byte-identical rect in book and paged mode,
and a wagon that reaches the window top parks there with the window as its
box while `overflow: clip` is active. Both facts are re-proved on every
`node test/run.js`.

**Six defects the plan did not foresee**, all found by writing the gates:

1. *Measuring the root while the clamp is applied.* The end boundary is the
   root's own bottom edge, so a `measure()` that reads it with `height` set
   feeds the clamp into itself and the story shrinks on every refresh.
   `measure()` unapplies, reads, walks, reapplies.
2. *`pending` was not cleared after a commit.* A one-element lookahead with
   no reset makes the stop walk non-deterministic: a boundary count that
   wobbled between runs and element labels (`EM`, `H2`) that belonged to
   other stops. A non-stop must return before touching `pending`, and a
   commit must clear it.
3. *`stopY()` returning null cleared the walk's state.* The `return` from
   the middle of the helper also reset `pending`, on exactly the path a
   `<br>`-only paragraph takes.
4. *Growing the boundary buffer reallocated it.* `new Float64Array(n * 2)`
   zeroed the entries already written; a second walk hid it in the browser,
   the single-measure fake page lost the whole list.
5. *`next()` and `prev()` moved `k` without `kEl`.* A source edit above the
   reader then put them one page back. The page is kept by element; every
   path that sets `k` sets `kEl`.
6. *The fake DOM's parser never advanced past a tag*, so inter-tag text
   carried the previous tag's source and a `<p></p>` was not empty. Every
   merge expectation in the node gate was wrong by one page because of it,
   and no existing gate noticed: a stray text node has zero height.

**Where the plan was wrong about the walking rules.** "A stop element starts
a new page at its top edge" is right, but a stop is only a page if its
portion has content. Empty `<p>`, the first of two `<br>`, a trailing `<br>`
before `</p>`, a `<section>` opening straight into its first `<p>` and a
blank opening page are one page each, not five. The one exception is a
picture: a `.snow-bg` counts as content, so a section that opens with a
background is a page of its own. This is now the rule, and the node gate
asserts all six cases by exact boundary list.

**The harness work is not built, on purpose.** The plan's "Harness & QA"
section is a second consumer of a feature that now has one real consumer
(`demo-paged.html`), its own fake-DOM gate and its own browser gate. Wiring
the QA page — a `paged` checkbox, a hash flag, autoscroll stepping,
generator `breaks`, a `qPaged` probe in `qaAll`, a diagnostics line — is
worth doing when someone next works on the QA page, not as part of shipping
the mode; until then it is the one unchecked criterion, marked as such.

**The browser gate asserts parking by scanning, not by `pos === 0`.** With
back-to-back full-window wagons, `chain()` puts a middle wagon at the top of
the window only inside a band about a pixel wide, and Chromium's integer
scroll lands on its edge. The gate scans the scroll range the clamp allows
and takes the closest approach, then asserts the box is the window and the
boundary is below the wagon's bottom.

**Accepted trade-offs.** Boundaries are cached in a `Float64Array` and
rebuilt only on measure; `y` is the raw buffer, so read `[0, count)`. The
controller has no scroll listener, so the host that wants a page counter
drives `frame(sY)` itself. `stepMax = 0.9` is a hard constant, not a setting:
a taller cap makes the browser's smooth scroll skip an event threshold.

**A page is one portion, and that changed the model, not the mechanism.**
The first implementation revealed *up to* a stop and sat the reader at the
new frontier, which bottom-aligned the boundary. Every page after the first
therefore showed a windowful of text the reader had already read, and the
mode read as a page that grows by one paragraph — the opposite of what a
visual novel does. The model is now: a page is the author's own portion
between two stops, the window's **top** is that portion's start, and three
things follow from it that the bottom-aligned model never had to answer.

1. **A short portion needs room to be read at all.** With the reader at the
   portion's start, the document has to be at least a window taller than the
   portion or the reader cannot get there, so the root also gets
   `margin-bottom: band(k)`. Margin, not padding: padding is inside the clip
   and would paint the unrevealed text under it. Not a spacer element
   either — the controller never touches the author's DOM. Measured in
   Chromium: a bottom margin on the last in-flow child does extend the
   document's scrollable area, and the band's own area paints the page
   background with no story content in it, so "blank paper under a short
   page" is a consequence of the geometry rather than a special case.
2. **`stepMax` had to become 1.** Consecutive pages start a window apart, so
   a cap below a window made every single turn cost two taps. At 1 a turn is
   always exactly one tap — the reader is at the old page's `spot`, and the
   new page begins at most a window below it — and only a page taller than
   the window needs extra taps, which is what the hurry branch is for.
3. **A page's own paper is the unit of art.** The engine paints a full-window
   wagon at the window's top, so a page shorter than the window ends in the
   middle of it and the cover is sliced. That is the author's page rhythm
   rather than a controller defect: the demo's art chapters are written as
   full-window pages (a cover page, a chapter card, full-window paragraphs,
   one paragraph taller than the window that has to be walked), and its
   text-only chapter keeps its pages as small as the prose in them, which is
   where the band is visible. The browser gate asserts the invariant the
   demo can honour: no wagon's box straddles a page's edge on any page of a
   full pass.

Three other ways to hide the text that came before were measured or reasoned
about and rejected, because each of them breaks the premise:

- **A negative top margin on the story root** would move every anchor, so
  every turn would need `Snowfall.refresh()` — re-arming events, prompts and
  game seats on each page turn.
- **`transform: translateY` on the root** would move the sticky scrollport
  and break wagon parking outright.
- **A bottom spacer inside the clip** would paint the unrevealed text it is
  supposed to hide.
- **Bottom-anchored pages with the old text clipped away** are impossible: a
  portion only reaches the window's top if it is at least a window tall.

**Two gate-level lessons from the rewrite.** Which portion is on the page
cannot be asserted from the *boxes* of the stop elements — a container
legitimately spans the page, and the last boundary is the story root itself —
so the gates assert the geometry the model is made of: `scrollY` against
`start(k)` and `spot(k)`, and `elementFromPoint` for what is actually painted
under a short page. And a minigame seats itself the first time it is looked
at, so its panel changes the height of the story below it: any check that
compares a measurement taken before a walk with one taken after needs two
refreshes at the same scroll to settle, or it compares a page that is still
being built.

**The first reader-facing defect: a story that changes its own height turned
every page after it.** The clamp model was right and every gate was green,
but a human reading `demo-paged.html` from the top saw the chapter-card page
open 160-odd pixels into the previous chapter's tail — the text that came
before still on the page — and from there every page landed a seat's height
off. The cause was the mechanism's own premise holding too well: the clamp
pins the root's box, so a game seating itself *inside* the root changes
nothing the document's height could reveal, and the boundary list — measured
once at boot — silently stopped describing the story it was cutting. The
gates could not see it because they compared the reader against the
controller's own `y` list, which is self-consistent even when it is wrong:
`scrollY == start(k)` is true for both the reader and the stale model at the
same time. What the reader actually gets is only visible against the
document: at an arrival, the window top must sit on the page's own stop
element's *measured* top. That ground-truth assertion is now in the browser
gate (per tap), in the demo's self-test (t3's sync walk and t5's walk, which
gives every tap a tick the way a reader does, and is therefore async), and
it is what caught and still proves the fix.

The fix is the two guards of the Decisions above, plus three specifics the
plan had no reason to predict:

1. *Scroll anchoring cancels a travelling programmatic smooth scroll.* When a
   seat unmounts above the viewport mid-glide, the browser's compensation
   kills the animation; a controller that waits for its own scroll to arrive
   before correcting waits forever, one page behind. Correcting mid-glide by
   re-targeting — a second `scrollTo` restarts the glide from wherever the
   browser left it — is what makes a smooth paged walk survive the demo's
   own seats. (Snap, not glide, when the reader is settled: the layout under
   them already jumped.)
2. *`prev()`'s smooth branch decremented `k` without `kEl`* — the review's
   own defect #5 rule, broken on one path. Harmless while `measure()` only
   ran on engine refresh; fatal the day a turn re-measures, because the
   re-measure restored the page from the stale element and the decrement
   never stuck (`while (prev())` never ended). Every path that sets `k` sets
   `kEl`, now also mid-glide, and the pending page is resolved by element
   too.
3. *The node gate's observer seam.* The fake DOM has no `MutationObserver`
   and no timer the page can see, so the gate installs a fake observer (the
   controller's own watch) and a fake timer, and fires both by hand: the
   real controller then runs the real path — burst, coalesced tick, walk,
   re-anchor — under the gate's control.

Two smaller repairs came with them: `t4` in the demo's self-test compared
the paged paper with the book document across the tick in which a walked-past
game drops its panel, so it now settles first (its own `settle()` lesson,
applied); and the self-test's paged walk awaited a tick per tap so it walks
the same reader path a human does — a synchronous walk cannot see a
scheduled re-measure, which is precisely why the original green self-test
never contradicted the broken reader experience.
